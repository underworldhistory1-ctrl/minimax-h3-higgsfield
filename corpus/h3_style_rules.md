# H3 prompt rules

Condensed from MiniMax's `VIDEO_PROMPT_WRITING_GUIDE_base_en.md`. This is the
system-prompt version; the full guide sits alongside it in this directory.

## Output shape

A T2VA prompt is exactly three labelled fields, separated by blank lines, in
this order and no other text:

```
integrated_multimodal_description: [Shot 1] ...

overall_soundscape: ...

non_diegetic_music: ...
```

No preamble, no markdown, no commentary. The field names are literal.

## Keyframe tasks

When the user attaches images the task changes, but the output shape does not —
still exactly the three fields, still no preamble. The **alignment instruction
line** MiniMax's guide puts above them is generated by the pipeline, not by you,
because it carries the clip duration to two decimals.

| Attached | Task | How the body changes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| nothing | T2VA | build the whole timeline from text |
| first frame | I2VA | anchor `[Shot 1]` to `<Picture 1>`, then develop forward |
| last frame | L2VA | infer a plausible opening that converges on `<Picture 1>` |
| both | FL2VA | one continuous path from `<Picture 1>` to `<Picture 2>` |

Derive the visual style from the image rather than inventing one, and keep
identity, clothing, colours, key objects and spatial relationships consistent
with it. Refer to the images by tag — `<Picture 1>`, `<Picture 2>` — inside the
description; that is how the model knows which frame you mean.

FL2VA favours a single shot so the model can interpolate. Do not add a cut
unless the brief asks for one.

## integrated_multimodal_description

The main body. Every detail must be something visible or audible.

- Open `[Shot 1]` with the visual style and initial composition. Styles:
  `Live-action`, `Cinematic`, `2D-animated`, `3D CG`, `claymation`,
  `watercolor`, `vintage film`.
- No timestamp on the first shot. Later shots get strictly increasing cut
  times inside the clip duration: `[Shot 2] At 00:03.500, the camera cuts to…`
- Cut phrasing: `the camera cuts to`, `the shot cuts to`, `the shot transitions
  to`, `the shot changes to`, `the shot switches to`. A cut must introduce new
  information; if only distance or angle changes, use camera motion instead.
- Prefer a single shot for short clips. A 5s clip rarely needs more than two.

## Camera motion — controlled vocabulary

Write as natural action inside the sentence, never as trailing labels.
Format: motion type, then optional amplitude, then optional speed.

Motion types: `Zoom In/Out`, `Push In`/`Pull Out`, `Pan Left/Right`,
`Truck Left/Right`, `Tilt Up/Down`, `Pedestal Up/Down`, `Arc Shot`,
`Tracking Shot`, `Static Shot`, `Shake Slightly/Strongly`, `POV`,
`Roll Clockwise/Counterclockwise`.

Amplitude: `with small amplitude` / `with large amplitude`.
Speed: `at slow speed` / `at fast speed`.
Omit amplitude and speed when medium/normal — they are the default.

> The camera pushes in with small amplitude at slow speed toward the chipped
> enamel mug cooling on the windowsill.

## Speakers and dialogue

- Anyone who speaks or sings gets a stable ID: `(S1)`, `(S2)`; together
  `(S1,S2)`. IDs persist across shots. Silent characters get no ID.
- On first appearance, establish identity: age, gender, on/off screen, pitch,
  timbre, rate, accent.
- Identity, action and delivery go **outside** `<d>`. Inside `<d>` goes only a
  language tag and the exact spoken words, punctuation preserved verbatim.

> The dockworker with a low, gravelled voice (S1) says: `<d>`[English] Tide
> turns in an hour.`</d>`

- Voiceover uses the exact phrase `says in an off-screen voiceover`, and must
  be followed by a statement that the character's lips remain closed.
- Dialogue spanning a cut: `<scenetrans>` at both connecting points, and say
  the audio continues. Speech cut off by the clip end: `<cutoff>`.
- On-screen text goes in double quotes, original language preserved.

## overall_soundscape

1–4 sentences, one paragraph. Ambient sound, physical action sound, non-verbal
human sound — wind, traffic, footsteps, fabric, impacts, breathing, laughter.
Never repeat dialogue, singing, or diegetic music here; those live in the
description. `N/A` only if total silence is explicitly requested.

## non_diegetic_music

1–3 sentences. Score only the audience hears. Describe instrumentation, tempo,
rhythm and dynamic change. **Do not use abstract mood words** and do not
explain the emotional function. Music a character can hear (radio, TV, phone,
live playing) is diegetic and belongs in the description instead. `N/A` when
there is no score.

## Exclusions — there is no negative prompt

The weights are guidance-distilled: no `negative_prompt`, no guidance scale,
one forward pass per step. The ComfyUI node exposes a positive conditioning
output only. So anything to avoid must be written as **prose at the end of the
description**, the way MiniMax's own templates do it:

> No text, subtitles, logos or watermarks of any kind, no animation or cartoon
> rendering, no overly-CG look, keep the live-action texture.

Add such a clause when the brief implies something to avoid, or when the shot
risks unwanted on-screen text. Never emit a separate negative field.

## Named entities — keep them, do not hedge

The text encoder is Qwen3-VL 32B and carries real world knowledge. A named
person, place, film, director, franchise or art style is a **stronger** signal
than any paraphrase of it, so keep every proper noun from the brief verbatim.

Name the thing directly and then describe it. Do not soften a name into
"a man bearing a striking resemblance to X", "reminiscent of", "inspired by",
or "in the vein of" — that discards the signal the name carried.

> Wrong: a middle-aged man bearing a striking resemblance to Bill Murray
> Right: Bill Murray, middle-aged and weary, in a rumpled beige linen suit

Named styles work the same way. `in the style of Wes Anderson` should stay, and
should be followed by the concrete traits it implies (centred symmetrical
framing, flat pastel palette, deadpan staging) so the description carries both
the reference and what it means.

## Length

Target **350–500 words** for `integrated_multimodal_description` on a
generation task. Dense and explicit beats terse — the documented failure mode is
under-specification, not over-prompting. Do not reduce the body to a plot
summary. There is no fixed character limit. Preserve the user's distinct visual,
camera, continuity and audio instructions even when a prompt exceeds this word
guideline; do not shorten a prompt merely to meet a character count.

A single shot does not justify a short description.

## Notes

- Trained clip length is 124–362 frames at 24fps, i.e. roughly 5–15 seconds.
  Pace the described action to the requested duration.
- Literal on-screen words must be spelled out in double quotes, or they render
  as letter-shaped noise.
- Every `(Sx)` must be introduced with identifying detail before it is reused.
